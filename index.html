    About Captain’s Log

    Captain’s Log is a personal daily companion: a journal for the day, the Modern Era Calendar as a dating tool, local sky weather, and the enterable Aretoria portal (also available as its own site). Nothing here syncs to Apple Notes or another device — entries stay in this browser; use “Copy text” to paste elsewhere.

    The Modern Era Calendar is a designed civil calendar. It is not nationally adopted. It keeps the Gregorian year length and leap rule, replaces uneven months with twelve months of thirty days, replaces the seven-day week with a ten-day cycle, and parks leftover days as named Renaissance Days.

    Epoch: July 4, 1776 = Primaday, July 1, Year 1

    For app display, Gregorian year Y maps to MEC Year (Y − 1776). January 1 of each Gregorian year aligns with January 1, Primaday of that MEC year. Day-to-day conversion uses the Section 5 slot algorithm — never a fixed lag.

    Structure

    • 12 months of exactly 30 regular days each
    • 3 cycles of 10 days per month (Cycle 1: 1–10, Cycle 2: 11–20, Cycle 3: 21–30)
    • Day names: Primaday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Quartiday, Funfday, Viday, Sunday, Octiday, Noviday, Centiday (rest day)
    • Centiday is the rest day — the 10th day of every cycle (days 10, 20, and 30 of each month)
    • Common year: 360 + 5 Renaissance Days = 365
    • Leap year: + Hermes Trismegistus Day after October = 366

    Renaissance Days

    • Local Area Network Day — after January
    • Global Society Day — after March
    • Nature Harmony Day — after May
    • Tesla Day — after July
    • Theology Day — after August
    • Hermes Trismegistus Day — after October (leap years only)

    Holiday markers

    Amber chips mark major bank holidays and widely observed festivals on the underlying Gregorian date (US, UK/Commonwealth, Canada, Mexico, Christian, Jewish, Islamic Eids, plus Chinese New Year and Diwali where a lookup covers the year). Rose chips are MEC Renaissance Days, and gold marks Centiday, the rest day. Each amber chip names its origin (Jewish, Christian, US civil, UK bank, and so on). Jewish dates are computed from the Hebrew calendar and mark the first full day (observance starts the evening before); they follow the Israeli one-day festival calendar used by the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange. Eid dates come from a fixed Saudi Umm al-Qura table for 2024–2037 only (no Eid markers outside that range); local moon sighting can shift a day.

    When a Gregorian holiday is selected (day detail, converter, or Today), MEC shows a short bank status — open or closed, and which areas are affected (e.g. “Banks closed in the US and UK”). Early-close days note that as well.

    • Fixed / civil: New Year’s, Epiphany, MLK, Valentine’s, Presidents’, Women’s Day, St Patrick’s, Earth Day, Cinco de Mayo, Mother’s/Father’s, Juneteenth, Canada Day, US Independence, Mexican Independence, UN Day, Halloween, Día de los Muertos, Veterans/Remembrance, US Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve/Day, Boxing Day/Kwanzaa, NYE
    • Bank-style: Good Friday, Easter Monday, Early May / Spring / Summer Bank Holidays, Victoria Day, Canadian Thanksgiving
    • Christian (from Easter): Ash Wednesday, Easter, Ascension, Pentecost
    • Jewish (Hebrew calendar): Purim, Erev Passover, Passover (1st and 7th days), Shavuot, Tisha B’Av, Erev Rosh Hashanah, Rosh Hashanah (Days 1–2), Erev Yom Kippur, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret / Simchat Torah, Hanukkah (US and Israeli banks open)
    • Islamic (Umm al-Qura table, 2024–2037): Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha
    • Lookups (~2024–2035/37): Chinese New Year, Diwali

    Local sky

    The strip above Today shows the place, current conditions and temperature (°F) from Open-Meteo (free, no key). It defaults to St. George, UT. Tap “Use my location” to let your browser share your position; the weather then follows you. Your coordinates stay in this browser and are only sent to the weather and place-name lookups. If you deny permission or are offline, the last reading (or St. George) is shown instead.

    The soft landscape band at the top of the Calendar view is painted from that same reading — no extra lookups. Red-rock desert country (like St. George) gets mesas, golden grass, dust and heat shimmer; elsewhere a meadow with seasonal trees. Clouds, rain, snow, fog and storms follow the current conditions, and the sun, golden hour and night follow the sun’s actual position. With no weather reading it falls back to a gentle seasonal scene. If your device asks for reduced motion, the scene is a still picture.

    Captain’s Log & Aretoria

    On wide screens the Calendar view shows three columns: the calendar, a blank Captain’s Log for the day, and the Aretoria portal. On phones they stack in that order. Log entries and realm reflections are saved only in this browser (one entry per Gregorian date). They are not synced to Apple Notes or any other device; use “Copy text” to paste an entry elsewhere.

    Enter the Realms opens Aretoria full screen: a shrine fly-through into the Axial hub (its own painted floating-island backdrop), seven realm environments (Wisdom, Courage, Humanity, Justice, Temperance, Transcendence and Shadow), each with a painted realm scene and its Guardian’s painted portrait (drawn figures remain the fallback), Irishnu the Guide at the axis, the virtue advisors (portraits from the Aretoria note, with generated art for the rest), a Hall of Virtues and the Creed. The advisors are scripted for now: no AI and no server. Their questions save a short answer in this browser, under the same per-day realm reflections. The opening and closing lines wait for you: tap or click anywhere, or press Enter or Space, to continue. Esc or ✕ leaves. Aretoria loads only when you enter it, and it honors reduced motion. You can also open the standalone Aretoria app at casswaters.github.io/aretoria.

    Install

    This app is an installable PWA. Use your browser’s “Add to Home Screen” or the install button in the header when available. Offline caching is provided by a service worker.
